# India payouts, tax and compliance for an India-based solo creator earning from foreign platforms (2025–2026)

Research date: 18 Nov 2026. Method: web search plus page fetches. Fetches of icai.org and taxguru.in failed because those hosts are outside this session's network allowlist, so ICAI material comes from search snippets and secondary sites. Many fee and payout details come from Indian cross-border payment companies (Skydo, Xflow, Winvesta, Karbon), which compete with PayPal, Payoneer and Stripe. Treat their fee numbers as indicative.

## 1. Payout routes by platform: who pays India directly, who needs Payoneer or PayPal, who doesn't support India; fees and FX

### Takeaway
Google (AdSense/YouTube) pays Indian creators by USD SWIFT wire to an Indian bank, and Gumroad pays INR straight to an Indian bank. Etsy pays Indian sellers only through Payoneer. Udemy and Adobe Stock pay through PayPal, Payoneer, Tipalti or Skrill. Stripe India is invite-only. Lemon Squeezy bank payouts to India depend on that Stripe approval, so PayPal is the usual fallback. Paddle's route to India is unclear. PayPal is the costliest channel, at about 4.4% + $0.30 plus a 3–4% FX markup. Payoneer is cheaper, at about 1% receiving plus 2–3% FX on withdrawal.

### Cited Findings
**Platform by platform**
- **Google AdSense / YouTube:** paid as a USD SWIFT wire to the registered Indian bank account. Arrival takes about 3–7 business days. The form asks for account holder name, bank name, IFSC, SWIFT code and account number. — [Xflow: Get paid from AdSense in India](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/get-paid-from-adsense-in-india); [Karbon: AdSense India payments](https://www.karboncard.com/blog/youtube-adsense-india-payments)
- **Gumroad:** "We do not pay out creators via Payoneer or Transferwise… if you were to set up a Payoneer account in your payouts settings, we would not be able to pay you." India is on Gumroad's bank-payout list in INR, and Gumroad pays only to a local bank account in local currency. — [Gumroad Help: Payoneer and Gumroad](https://gumroad.com/help/article/223-payoneer-and-gumroad); [Gumroad Help: Getting paid](https://gumroad.com/help/article/13-getting-paid)
- **Gumroad minimum:** third-party guides put it at $100, with bank arrival in about 2–7 business days. I did not confirm this on Gumroad's own pages. — [Winvesta: Gumroad payouts to India](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/freelancers/gumroad-payouts-india-fees-minimum-timing)
- **Etsy:** Indian sellers are paid only in USD to a Payoneer account, every Monday at 11:30am ET. Etsy cannot deposit in another currency. Payoneer then converts to INR and withdraws to the Indian bank, which takes 2–5 business days. — [Etsy Help: How to accept payments as a seller in India](https://help.etsy.com/hc/articles/6742925359255); [Etsy Help: Payoneer with Etsy Payments](https://help.etsy.com/hc/articles/16999319005207)
- **Etsy onboarding of new Indian shops is contested.** One guide says Etsy resumed onboarding Indian sellers in June 2025. Others report restrictions. — [ShipGlobal: Etsy new sellers](https://shipglobal.in/blogs/etsy-new-sellers/); [Skydo: Etsy](https://www.skydo.com/receive-international-payments-in-india/from-etsy)
- **Udemy:** Indian instructors choose PayPal, Payoneer or Tipalti, and a W-8BEN is required. Udemy pays within the first 10 business days of the following month. — [Xflow: Get paid from Udemy in India](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/get-paid-from-udemy-india); [Udemy community](https://community.udemy.com/en/discussion/161790/payout-methods-gateway-charges); [Udemy support](https://support.udemy.com/hc/articles/29780701031831)
- **Adobe Stock:** pays to PayPal, Payoneer or Skrill, with a $25 payout threshold. Payoneer is available to contributors outside the US. — [Adobe Help: Payment requirements for Contributors](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/payments-earnings/payment-taxes/payment-requirements.html)
- **Stripe India:** "invite only". New Indian businesses can't sign up through the website. Accepting international payments needs a valid Import/Export Code (IEC). India accounts accept international card payments in 135+ currencies. — [Stripe Docs: Accept international payments from India](https://docs.stripe.com/india-exports); [Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/accepting-international-payments-from-stripe-accounts-in-india)
- Third-party sites date the Stripe restriction to May 2024. Not confirmed on Stripe's own pages. — [Winvesta: Stripe alternatives](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/stripe-alternatives-for-india-best-payment-platforms-for-freelancers-and-businesses)
- **Stripe fee claims from a competitor blog:** about 4.3% on international cards, plus 2% conversion, plus 18% GST on fees. — [Xflow: Stripe transaction fees](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/stripe-transaction-fees)
- **Lemon Squeezy (merchant of record):** bank payouts to India run through Stripe and need the account to be pre-approved by Stripe. Otherwise the payout falls back to PayPal, at roughly $15–30 per payout in the example given. Lemon Squeezy charges 1% on international bank payouts. Reports describe onboarding delays and rejections for Indian merchants. — [Winvesta: Lemon Squeezy payouts India](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/lemon-squeezy-payouts-india-paypal-bank-fees); [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/lemonsqueezy-alternative-for-indian-saas-accepting-global-payments/)
- **Paddle (merchant of record):** Paddle's payout help pages don't name India or INR. One blog reads this as a SWIFT wire with a fixed fee, plus a conversion margin of up to 1.5% when the payout currency differs from the balance currency. — [Winvesta: Paddle payouts to India](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/paddle-payouts-india-fees-timing-bank-options)
- **Indian merchant-of-record alternatives:** Dodo Payments and Razorpay are pitched for Indian SaaS sellers. These claims come from vendor blogs. — [Dodo Payments blog](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/top-merchant-of-record-for-saas-india)

**Fees and FX (indicative)**
- **PayPal:** 4.4% + $0.30 per international commercial payment, a ~3–4% currency conversion markup, and 18% GST on fees. The PayPal account name must match the PAN. — [Xflow: PayPal USD→INR charges](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/how-much-paypal-charges-for-usd-to-inr); [Skydo: PayPal vs Payoneer](https://www.skydo.com/compare/paypal-vs-payoneer)
- **Payoneer:** about 1% ACH receiving fee, plus 2–3% conversion on withdrawal to an Indian bank. Free digital FIRA issued 24–72 hours after payment. — [Skydo: PayPal vs Payoneer](https://www.skydo.com/compare/paypal-vs-payoneer); [Skydo: Wise vs Payoneer](https://www.skydo.com/compare/wise-vs-payoneer)

### Inferences
- **Cheapest routes:** AdSense wire and Gumroad direct bank deposit avoid aggregator fees. The cost there is the Indian bank's FX spread and inward-remittance charges.
- **Etsy, Udemy and Adobe Stock:** Payoneer is usually cheaper than PayPal.
- **Indian digital-product or SaaS sellers:** Gumroad, or an India-friendly merchant of record, is more practical than Stripe direct or Lemon Squeezy.
- **Amazon KDP, Apple App Store, Google Play and Meta:** my background knowledge, not verified in this session, is that all four pay Indian bank accounts directly by EFT or wire. Confirm on each platform's payments help page.

### Gaps
- Not verified from primary pages: Meta (Facebook/Instagram) creator payouts to India, the Apple App Store payout method and minimum, Google Play payout method, and Amazon KDP's India EFT or wire option and threshold.
- Wise's current support for Indian residents receiving business payments was not researched. My background knowledge is that Wise's India entity has restrictions on business receipts.
- I found no primary source for Gumroad, Udemy or Adobe Stock fee levels.

## 2. US tax withholding: W-8BEN, India–US DTAA rates, claiming treaty benefit, Form 67 foreign tax credit

### Takeaway
- **YouTube (US viewers):** Google withholds 24% of total YouTube earnings if no tax info is on file. With a W-8BEN claiming the India treaty, it withholds 15% on US-viewer earnings only. Without a treaty claim, the rate is 30%.
- **Royalties (KDP, stock):** the default is 30%. The India–US treaty Article 12 rate for copyright royalties is 15%.
- **Getting credit in India:** US tax withheld is claimed as a foreign tax credit in India by filing Form 67.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube statement on the treaty:** "India and the U.S. have a tax treaty relationship that reduces the tax rate to 15 per cent of earnings from viewers in the US." — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/youtube-creators-in-india-have-to-pay-withholding-tax-on-us-earnings-111538.html)
- **No form submitted:** Google may withhold up to 24% of total worldwide YouTube earnings. A valid form without a treaty claim means 30% on US-viewer earnings. — [Google YouTube help thread](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/101463270); [vidIQ guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/non-u-s-creators-24-percent-tax-youtube-revenue/); [air.io](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-tax-info-w-8ben)
- **W-8BEN validity:** about 3 years; renew before it expires. — [air.io](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-tax-info-w-8ben)
- **KDP default:** 30% US withholding for non-US persons on Amazon.com eBook sales and some print sales, unless a treaty claim with a tax identification number is made in KDP's tax interview. — [Vappingo: KDP for non-US authors](https://www.vappingo.com/word-blog/?p=11572)
- **KDP treaty claim:** the IRS treaty table lists India at 15% for copyright royalties (Art. 12). Cite India, Article 12, 15% in W-8BEN Part II. — [Winvesta W-8BEN guide](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/investors/w-8ben-form-for-indians-complete-guide-to-filling-and-filing); [Xflow W-8BEN vs W-8BEN-E](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/form-w-8-ben-and-w-8-ben-e)
- **KDP on Amazon.in:** one guide says 18% GST is deducted from the list price before royalty is computed. — [kdpindia.com FAQ](https://kdpindia.com/faq/)
- **Form 67:** US tax withheld can be credited against Indian tax by filing Form 67 on the Income Tax e-filing portal. — [Winvesta W-8BEN guide](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/investors/w-8ben-form-for-indians-complete-guide-to-filling-and-filing); [Karbon: platform withholding](https://www.karboncard.com/blog/platform-withholding-us-india-freelancers)
- **Withholding mechanism:** AdSense and YouTube withholding is applied to "royalty" style income from US viewers. — [Karbon: YouTube AdSense withholding India](https://www.karboncard.com/blog/youtube-adsense-tax-withholding-india)

### Inferences
- **Indian TIN:** the PAN serves as the foreign TIN on the W-8BEN.
- **Under the treaty:** the 15% rate applies to royalties (YouTube, KDP, stock). Pure services income from US clients is generally business profits or independent personal services (Art. 7/15), so 0% US withholding is the usual position. Several guides refer to Art. 15 for freelancers.
- **Form 67 timing:** it is generally filed before or with the ITR for the relevant year. Credit is limited to the lower of the US tax and the Indian tax on that income. Background knowledge (Rule 128): the old Rule 128 cross-referencing changes to the new Act's rules from tax year 2026-27, so check the updated form number under the Income-tax Rules 2026.
- **Effective cost:** if the creator's Indian slab rate on the income exceeds 15%, the treaty withholding is fully creditable and the net extra cost is about zero. At low slabs or under the new regime's rebate, part of the credit can be wasted.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed on primary pages: Google's AdSense tax help page or the IRS Publication 515 / treaty table. The rates above are consistent across secondary sources.
- Withholding by Apple, Google Play, Meta, Udemy and Adobe Stock was not researched individually.
- No primary source found for the Form 67 procedure under the Income-tax Act 2025 or Rules 2026.

## 3. GST: export of services, LUT, ₹20 lakh threshold, OIDAR and intermediary issues, Google Play and App Store sales to Indian users

### Takeaway
- **Export status:** income from foreign platforms (AdSense, sales to foreign buyers) is widely treated as a zero-rated export of services.
- **Threshold and LUT:** the ₹20 lakh threshold (₹10 lakh in special-category states) is measured on aggregate turnover including exports. Below it, registration is optional, but registration is needed to export under LUT (Form RFD-11, filed annually).
- **Play Store:** Google acts as marketplace provider and remits GST on Play sales to Indian buyers, and it collects TCS from GST-registered Indian developers.
- **App Store:** Apple's position for Indian sales was not confirmed on a primary page.

### Cited Findings
- **Export of services:** a supply is an export where the supplier is in India and the recipient and place of supply are outside India. Some commentary characterises YouTube ad revenue as OIDAR, with place of supply abroad, making it an export of services. — [ClearTax: GST on YouTube income](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-youtube-income); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=971302)
- **LUT:** exports can be made under bond or LUT in Form RFD-11 without paying IGST, under Rule 96A of the CGST Rules. Alternatively, pay IGST and claim a refund. The LUT is filed afresh for each financial year. — [TaxGuru: GST for freelancers](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-freelancers-consultants-guide-domestic-foreign-clients.html); [Skydo: Export of services under GST](https://www.skydo.com/blog/export-of-services-under-gst)
- **Threshold:** ₹20 lakh for services (₹10 lakh in special-category states), on aggregate turnover including export of services. Inter-state service suppliers below the threshold are exempt from registration under Notification 10/2017-IGST. Without registration you cannot export under LUT or claim ITC refunds. — [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/gst-registration-freelancers-export-services-below-20-lakhs); [Xflow: GST for freelancers](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/gst-for-freelancers)
- **Conflicting creator guidance:** one blog says AdSense-only creators need not register. Another says registration is mandatory once total income passes ₹20 lakh. — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-youtube-income); [TheMunim](https://themunim.com/?p=52106)
- **Google Play (Google's own help pages):**
  - Google is responsible as marketplace provider for charging, collecting and remitting GST on paid apps and in-app purchases bought by customers in India, and deducts that GST from developer revenue.
  - Indian developers who must register have to give Google their GSTIN so it can withhold GST TCS on GST-liable sales.
  - Play prices must include GST, which reduces India revenue by about 18%.
  - Another Google page says India-address developers "may be responsible for paying GST" on sales to Indian customers. The pages conflict.
  - Sources: [Google Play Console Help 9384608](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9384608); [Google Payments Center 7421525](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7421525); [Google: Tax info for India](https://support.google.com/product-documentation/answer/15152449)
- **Apple App Store:** a secondary blog says Apple acts as merchant of record and India GST of 18% is built into the price. Apple's terms say it "shall not collect and remit taxes for local Developers" in some territories. India-specific wording was not verified. — [appsops.store](https://appsops.store/blog/app-store-tax-vat-gst-territories); [Apple Schedule 2/3 Exhibits (Aug 2025)](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/exhibits/Exhibits-to-Schedule-2-and-3-20250821-English.pdf)

### Inferences
- **AdSense / YouTube:** Google Asia Pacific (Singapore) or Google LLC is the recipient, so export conditions are generally met when payment is received in convertible foreign exchange (or INR where RBI permits).
- **Intermediary risk:**
  - Section 13(8)(b) IGST sets place of supply in India for "intermediary" services.
  - It is a risk only where the creator arranges supplies between others, such as affiliate or referral commissions from foreign platforms.
  - Background knowledge, not verified here: the GST Council in 2025 recommended omitting 13(8)(b). Check whether the omission has been notified.
- **Marketplace sales to foreign buyers (Etsy, Gumroad, KDP to foreign customers):** generally exports.
- **Sales to Indian buyers through foreign marketplaces:**
  - These are domestic B2C supplies.
  - Section 24 CGST requires compulsory registration for persons supplying through an e-commerce operator that must collect TCS. Notification 65/2017 and later relief removed this for small service suppliers.
  - Google's TCS collection implies Google treats itself as an e-commerce operator for Indian developers.
- **OIDAR:** the OIDAR rules (IGST s.14) target foreign suppliers selling to Indian non-taxable recipients. For an Indian creator selling digital goods abroad, OIDAR classification mainly affects place of supply (location of recipient), which supports export treatment.

### Gaps
- No CBIC circular specific to YouTube or AdSense creators was found.
- The status of the 13(8)(b) intermediary amendment (GST Council 2025 recommendation / Finance Act 2026) is unverified.
- Apple's India GST role and whether Apple collects TCS from Indian developers were not verified.
- Whether Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy or Paddle (as merchant of record) charge Indian GST on Indian buyers was not researched.
- Whether payment via a PayPal or Payoneer INR conversion satisfies the "convertible foreign exchange" export condition is commonly accepted but no primary source was found.

## 4. Income tax: 44ADA vs 44AD for creators, new Income-tax Act 2025, advance tax, Equalisation Levy

### Takeaway
- **Presumptive regime:** content creation is not a listed "specified profession" for 44ADA. CBDT added ITR business code 16021 "Social Media Influencers" under the Profession category, but did not notify it as a specified profession, so most experts default to 44AD (business, 6%/8%).
- **New Act:** from 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act 2025 replaces the 1961 Act. 44AD, 44ADA and 44AE merge into Section 58 with limits unchanged (₹75 lakh / 50% for professions).
- **Equalisation Levy:** abolished. The 2% e-commerce levy went from 1 Aug 2024, and the 6% online-ads levy from 1 Apr 2025. It never applied to a creator's receipts anyway.

### Cited Findings
- **Code 16021:** CBDT added "Social Media Influencers" as code 16021 under the Profession category in the ITR utility. No CBDT notification or Finance Act amendment adds influencers to the specified-profession list. Experts say 44AD should apply, with income shown as business income. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/cbdt-introduces-new-itr-code-for-social-media-influencers-sparking-debate-among-tax-experts-1428531); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/influencers-now-have-a-dedicated-itr-code-heres-how-they-should-file-returns-146024.html); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/explained-the-new-tax-code-for-social-media-influencers-and-the-trouble-with-it-77132.htm)
- **Contrary claim:** one CA blog says CBDT clarified YouTubers qualify under 44ADA, without a primary citation. This contradicts the reporting above. — [casahuja.com](https://www.casahuja.com/2025/08/section-44ada-presumptive-taxation-for.html)
- **Presumptive rates:** 44ADA gives 50% deemed profit up to ₹75 lakh receipts. 44AD gives 8% (6% on digital receipts) up to ₹2 crore. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/personal-finance/tax/story/freelancers-youtubers-influencers-5-tax-traps-you-must-avoid-in-ay-2025-26-487923-2025-08-05); [Upstox ITR 2026 guide for creators](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/tax/itr-2026-tax-filing-guide-for-instagram-you-tube-x-and-linked-in-content-creators/article-198478/)
- **New Act and Section 58:** the Income-tax Act 2025 is effective 1 April 2026. Sections 44AD, 44ADA and 44AE merge into Section 58. Professionals' ₹75 lakh limit and 50% rate are unchanged (Section 58(1)(b)). Tax audit moves to Section 63. Books are required under 58(3) if lower income is declared and total income exceeds the basic exemption. — [TaxGuru: Section 58 analysis](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/analysis-presumptive-taxation-section-58-income-tax-act-2025.html); [Wisebooks](https://wisebooks.in/blog/section-58-of-the-income-tax-act-2025-complete-guide-to-the-new-presumptive-taxation-scheme-for-businesses-professionals-and-transport-operators); [NIRC-ICAI presentation (May 2026)](https://nirc.icai.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/The-New-Income-Tax-Act-2025-nanda-sir.pdf)
- **Equalisation Levy:**
  - The 6% levy on online advertising was removed by government amendments to the Finance Bill 2025, effective 1 April 2025. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/india-equalisation-levy-removed-tax-impact-2025-125040100810_1.html); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/03/equalisation-levy-online-advertisements-abolished-google-meta-x-ads)
  - The 2% e-commerce levy was withdrawn effective 1 Aug 2024. — [Xflow: Equalisation Levy 2026](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/equalisation-levy)
  - The levy applied to Indian payers' payments to non-residents. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/policy/story/equalisation-levy-of-6-on-online-ads-likely-to-be-scrapped-from-april-1-469169-2025-03-24)

### Inferences
- **For a CA:** 44ADA would apply naturally to CA professional fees ("accountancy" is a specified profession). Creator income from AdSense, digital products or KDP royalties is better treated as business income under 44AD/58, or under regular books.
- **Mixing schemes:** combining 44ADA and 44AD for different activities in the same year is debated. A conservative approach is regular books for the creator business.
- **Digital receipts:** foreign platform receipts arrive by bank or wire, so they count as digital receipts. Under 44AD that means the 6% rate, and the ₹3 crore enhanced limit applies if cash receipts are 5% or less (₹75 lakh for 44ADA).
- **Advance tax (background knowledge):** 15/45/75/100% by 15 Jun/Sep/Dec/Mar. Presumptive 44AD/44ADA taxpayers may pay 100% by 15 March. Not separately sourced here; check the corresponding provisions in the 2025 Act.
- **Terminology:** from 1 April 2026 the term is "tax year", replacing previous year / assessment year, under the new Act.
- **Equalisation Levy:** only relevant if the creator buys foreign ads, such as Meta or Google ads, from India. Since it has been abolished, no deduction is needed. Payments to Meta or Google Ads could still raise other questions such as TDS or GST reverse charge; not researched.

### Gaps
- No primary CBDT clarification on creators' eligibility for 44ADA or Section 58 was found.
- Budget 2026 changes affecting creators or digital receipts were not researched.
- New-Act section numbers for advance tax and Form 67 equivalents were not verified.

## 5. FEMA / RBI: purpose codes, FIRC/FIRA, e-BRC

### Takeaway
- **Purpose codes:** inward remittances must carry an RBI purpose code.
  - Royalties and licensing fall in group P09 (e.g., P0902).
  - Software is P0802, or P0807 for off-site software under SOFTEX.
  - Consulting is P1006.
  - Commercial guides conflict on codes for advertising income.
- **Export proof:** banks and Payoneer issue FIRA/FIRC as proof of export for GST and LUT purposes. PayPal's FIRA practice varies.

### Cited Findings
- **RBI purpose-code list:**
  - Group 09 is "Royalties & License Fees", including P0901 (franchises, patents) and P0902 (receipts for use, through licensing arrangements).
  - P0802 covers software implementation or consultancy not covered by SOFTEX.
  - P0807 covers off-site software exports.
  - Source: [RBI purpose code annexure (PDF)](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/39710850/52220.pdf); [Winvesta: P0807](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/p0807-purpose-code-complete-guide-for-indian-software-exporters)
- **Conflicting commercial guidance:** P1006 for consulting, management or PR; P1007 for marketing or research; P1009 for royalties on creative assets. The RBI table puts royalties in group 09. — [Karbon: RBI purpose codes for freelancers](https://www.karboncard.com/blog/rbi-purpose-codes-freelancers-india); [Skydo: RBI purpose codes](https://www.skydo.com/rbi-purpose-codes); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/rbi-purpose-code-remittance-compliance-guide/)
- **FIRA availability:**
  - Payoneer: free digital FIRA.
  - PayPal: weekly consolidated digital FIRA according to one source. Others say no automatic FIRA, or a manual FIRC at about ₹2,000.
  - Source: [Skydo: PayPal vs Payoneer](https://www.skydo.com/compare/paypal-vs-payoneer); [Skydo: PayPal for exporters](https://www.skydo.com/blog/paypal-business-account-for-indian-exporters)
- **Bank requirements:** banks need SOFTEX approval before issuing a BRC for software exports. — [Winvesta: P0807](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/p0807-purpose-code-your-complete-guide-for-off-site-software-exports)

### Inferences
- **Code choice:** YouTube AdSense is often coded by banks as advertising or other services. KDP and stock income fit royalties (P09xx). Digital product sales can fit P0802/P0807 for software or P1009 for other. The creator should confirm the code with the AD bank and keep it consistent with GST invoice descriptions.
- **Records to keep:** FIRA/FIRC or bank advice for each receipt, preferably downloaded monthly, to support zero-rated export claims in GSTR-1 (table 6A) and any ITC refund.
- **e-BRC (background knowledge, not verified here):** DGFT's e-BRC system lets banks upload realisation data. Since 2022–23, banks issue e-FIRC/e-BRC for services that can be pulled on the DGFT portal. It is mainly needed for Foreign Trade Policy benefits; GST export proof can rely on FIRA. An IEC is not required for most service exporters, except where Stripe or similar platforms require one.

### Gaps
- No primary DGFT or RBI source on e-BRC or e-FIRC for services was fetched.
- The RBI annexure version and date were not confirmed.
- No source found on what purpose code Google, Apple or Amazon use when wiring to Indian banks.

## 6. Issues for a CA in practice running such businesses (ICAI rules)

### Takeaway
- **The rule:** under Clause (11) of Part I of the First Schedule to the CA Act, 1949 and Regulation 190A, a CA in practice may not engage in any business or occupation other than accountancy without Council permission, unless it falls under the Council's general-permission list.
- **The general-permission list:** includes authorship of books and articles, private tutorship (up to 25 hours a week of teaching if attest work continues), and similar items.
- **Business activities** generally need specific permission and normally bar attest functions.
- **Enforcement:** ICAI has disciplined members for breaches.

### Cited Findings
- **Base rule:** Regulation 190A says no business or occupation other than accountancy may be taken up without prior ICAI permission. The violation is under Clause (11), Part I, First Schedule to the CA Act. — [Taxscan: CA practice with employment](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/ca-practice-with-full-time-employment-know-icai-restrictions-with-misconduct-cases-1439256); [ICAI resolution under Reg 190A (URL, not fetched)](https://www.icai.org/post/resolution-now-passed-by-the-council-under-regulation-190a)
- **General-permission examples:** "private tutorship", "author of books & articles", "holding public offices", "employment with CA firm", and part-time tutorship under ICAI coaching. Teaching is capped at 25 hours a week of direct teaching to remain eligible for attest functions. The list also allows any other business or occupation the Executive Committee permits from time to time. — [WIRC-ICAI Code of Ethics 2020 material](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/material/Code-of-Ethics-2020-MK.pdf); [WIRC Volume 5](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/announcement/Volume-5.pdf)
- **Attest restriction:** members engaged in other business or occupation under permission are ordinarily not entitled to perform attest functions. Some items, such as authorship, are exempt. — [WIRC Volume 5](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/announcement/Volume-5.pdf)

### Inferences
- **How each activity likely sits:**
  - Writing ebooks or KDP books likely falls under "authorship of books and articles", which has general permission and is attest-exempt.
  - Online courses (Udemy) or educational YouTube may fall under tutorship or coaching, subject to the hours cap and advertising and solicitation limits.
  - Selling templates, apps or SaaS, running an Etsy shop, or a monetised non-educational channel looks like "business", which needs specific Council permission. Having that permission would ordinarily end eligibility for attest work.
- **Content restrictions:** a CA creator should avoid content that solicits clients or advertises their practice, such as a "hire me for GST filing" call to action. That conflicts with Clause (6) and (7) of Part I of the First Schedule (solicitation and advertising), in addition to Regulation 190A.
- **Structure:** setting up an LLP or company to run the business does not escape the rule, since being a director or partner in a non-CA business is itself an occupation needing permission. Directorships without substantial interest have been on the general-permission list historically (background knowledge, not verified).
