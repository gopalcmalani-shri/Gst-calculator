# YouTube RPM and CPM by Content Niche (2025–2026), Geography, Shorts vs Long-form, Seasonality

**Method note (read first):** Research date: 18 Nov 2026. This session could not fetch pages directly. Fetches of outlierkit.com, air.io, influencermarketinghub.com and lenostube.com failed with a network/DNS block. Every figure below comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Treat each number as "as reported by that source". YouTube does not publish RPM by niche, so every table is a sample or an estimate. Sources disagree by 2–10x, and many "RPM" tables actually quote CPM, which is the advertiser price before YouTube's 45% cut. Kids/Made-for-Kids content is excluded throughout. The one exception is AIR's Kids median, kept only as a floor reference.

**Definitions:** CPM is what the advertiser pays per 1,000 ad impressions. RPM is what the creator earns per 1,000 total views, after YouTube's 45% cut. RPM also counts unmonetized views and includes Premium, membership and Super Chat revenue. RPM is therefore usually about 35–55% of CPM ([YoutubeNiches / Activids via search](https://youtubeniches.com/blog/real-cpm-data-by-niche-the-complete-youtube-monetization-framework-2026); [vloggingpro](https://vloggingpro.com/youtube-cpm/)). AIR found that the median channel had ads on only 53% of its views ([AIR Media-Tech 2026](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)).

## Q1. Typical RPM/CPM ranges by niche (Tier-1 / mostly US audiences, long-form)

### Takeaway
There are two kinds of source. The only measured multi-channel dataset is AIR Media-Tech: 300 channels, May 2025 to May 2026, with a long-form median RPM of about $2.30. Its niche medians are far lower than SEO-blog tables, roughly $1–$10 against $5–$50. In the blog tables, the order of niches is consistent: insurance/credit cards/finance/legal/tax/B2B-SaaS at the top ($10–$45 RPM), then tech/education/real estate/marketing, then true crime/history in the middle ($4–$9), then gaming/entertainment at the bottom ($1–$5). Use blog figures as upper-bound targets for well-optimized US-audience channels, not as typical outcomes.

### Cited Findings

Niche medians: Education & Science $10.22, Transport $5.69, Lifestyle $2.98, Entertainment $2.43, Gadgets & Tech $2.33, Music $2.28, Gaming $2.05, Business & Finance $2.01, Health & Sport $1.23, Kids & Teens $0.33. ### Inferences
- AIR's Business & Finance median ($2.01, under 10 channels) is far below blog finance ranges ($8–$50). The likely reasons are a small, non-US-heavy sample in AIR, and blogs quoting top performers or CPM. A realistic US-focused finance channel is probably in the $8–$25 RPM range, with $30+ reserved for credit card, insurance and tax content that targets high-intent US buyers.
- AIR's measured data puts Education & Science at the top, which suggests explainer/educational formats with long watch time and Tier-1 audiences out-earn their blog reputation.
- For credit cards, insurance and taxes, the CPM is high because affiliate and lead-gen advertisers bid aggressively. Fill rates are uneven, though, so realized RPM varies widely.

### Gaps

- No sourced figures for motivation/self-improvement or for standalone health.

## Q2. Geography: Tier-1 (US/UK/CA/AU) vs India; Hindi vs English; Indian finance/tax

### Takeaway
Audience country dominates. US CPM is roughly 15–20x Indian CPM: about $14.67 for the US against $0.74 for India in AIR's June 2025 data across 3,000+ channels. Indian-audience long-form RPM is typically ₹25–₹150 (about $0.30–$1.80) per 1k views, and finance sits at the top at about ₹50–₹250. English content with NRI or Tier-1 viewers earns several times more than Hindi content. No CA/tax-specific Indian channel data was found.

### Cited Findings
- AIR Media-Tech, June 2025 (3,000+ channels), CPM by country: US $14.67, Australia $13.30, UK $8.91, India $0.74, Pakistan $0.53 — [AIR via search](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-in-2025-10-countries-you-cant-ignore); also reported by [Digital Information World, Aug 2025](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/08/youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025-how-location.html)
- Other 2025 estimates: India CPM about $0.70–$2.00 against a US average of $15.34–$40.00. Another source gives a US median of $11.95 against $0.96 for India — [awisee](https://awisee.com/blog/cpm-rates-by-country/); [youtubetoolshub](https://www.youtubetoolshub.com/resources/youtube-cpm-rates)
- India typical CPM about $1.50 and RPM about $0.80 (RevenueLab, 2026) — [ytmoneycalculator country blog](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/youtube-cpm-rates-by-country/)
- Viewer country matters more than creator country. NRI viewers in the US/UK/CA/AU pay out at roughly $5–$12 RPM, against $0.30–$1.50 for Indian viewers — [youseo.app](https://youseo.app/youtube-monetization-india)
- A mostly Indian audience generally sees $0.20–$2 per 1,000 views across niches — [bosswallah / upgrowth via search](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-in-india-complete-guide/)
- Indian long-form RPM overall: ₹25–₹150 ([upGrowth 2026](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-india-guide-2026/)); ₹15–₹250 ([influencermetric](https://influencermetric.in/blog/how-much-do-youtubers-earn-india)). With a mostly US audience, the figure is about ₹670–₹1,340 per 1k ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-india-guide-2026/))
- **Indian finance:**
  - Long-form finance RPM ₹40–₹80; worked example of a Hindi finance channel at ₹50 base RPM — [fluxnote India finance RPM 2026](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-india-finance-rpm-2026)
  - Finance/stock market ₹80–₹250 RPM ([identitykit](https://www.identitykit.in/blog/youtube-rpm-india-niche-2026)); ₹90–₹250 ([influencermetric](https://influencermetric.in/blog/how-much-do-youtubers-earn-india)); "optimized finance/tech" ₹150 ([fluxnote India](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-income-india-2026))
  - Finance channels aimed at IT professionals and NRIs: CPM ₹200–₹500, because brokers and fund platforms bid aggressively — [fluxnote India income](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-income-india-2026)
  - Hindi finance about ₹1–3 lakh per 1M views. English finance aimed at NRIs earns more — [fluxnote finance channel India](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-finance-channel-india-2026)
  - Indian tech is about ₹70–₹180 RPM and music about ₹20–₹45 — [identitykit](https://www.identitykit.in/blog/youtube-rpm-india-niche-2026)
- **Hindi vs English:**
  - English about ₹80–₹250 vs Hindi ₹25–₹80 (quoted as CPM) — [identitykit](https://www.identitykit.in/blog/youtube-rpm-india-niche-2026)
  - Hinglish in between at ₹50–₹150 — [upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-in-india-complete-guide/)
  - Hindi finance RPM ₹50–₹200 vs English finance ₹400–₹600+ — [smartcalc](https://smartcalc.online/calculators/youtube_earnings.html)
  - English finance $2–$5 RPM vs Hindi personal finance $1.50–$4 — [ytmoneycalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/how-much-youtube-pay-per-1000-views/). This conflicts with the smartcalc gap, which is much larger.
  - Anecdote: an Indian tech creator who switched to English saw RPM go from about ₹85 to about $3.80. Sample size not given — [ytmoneycalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/how-much-youtube-pay-per-1000-views/)

### Inferences
- The US/India multiple is about 15–20x on CPM. On RPM within the same niche it is probably about 10–25x: US finance at $10–$25 against Indian Hindi finance at about ₹40–₹150 ($0.50–$1.80).
- For an Indian GST/tax/CA channel, the best available proxy is Indian finance: Hindi about ₹40–₹150 RPM, English about ₹100–₹400+ depending on NRI share. Q4 and tax-filing months (July and March for India) may lift these figures, but this is not sourced.
- Language matters mainly because it changes audience geography and income profile, not because of the language itself.

### Gaps
- No verified screenshot disclosures from named Indian finance or tax YouTubers were found.
- No CA/GST/income-tax-specific Indian RPM data was found.
- UK/Canada/Australia RPM by niche (rather than overall CPM) was not found. Canada CPM was not retrieved.

## Q3. Long-form vs Shorts RPM (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Shorts RPM is typically $0.03–$0.20 per 1k views. AIR's US average is about $0.33, and India is about $0.01. That is about 3–14% of long-form RPM. Long-form remains the monetization engine for high-RPM niches.

### Cited Findings
- AIR (274-channel Shorts sample, 2026): median Shorts RPM $0.07–$0.20 in most niches, with Music the exception at $0.97–$1.48. The niche range was $0.02–$1.48. By country, Shorts RPM ran from about $0.01 in India to $0.33 in the US. Shorts RPM is about 3–14% of long-form RPM in almost every niche — [AIR Shorts RPM 2026](https://air.io/en/monetization/what-rpm-can-you-expect-from-shorts-in-2026); [AIR 2026 stats](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)
- AIR 2025 Shorts RPM by country: US $0.328, Switzerland $0.205, Australia $0.193, South Korea $0.185, UK $0.166 — [AIR Shorts 2025](https://air.io/en/what-prm-can-you-expect-from-shorts-in-2025)
- US average Shorts RPM was about $0.07 in late 2025 (fluxnote citing Influencer Marketing Hub creator reports) — [fluxnote US Shorts](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-earnings-usa-2026); [IMH Shorts RPM benchmarks](https://influencermarketinghub.com/youtube-shorts-rpm/)
- Typical Shorts RPM about $0.05 vs long-form about $3 — [revid.ai](https://www.revid.ai/blog/how-much-do-youtubers-make)
- Planning tiers of 5–10, 10–25 or 25–50 cents per 1k depending on niche and audience — [saturaai](https://saturaai.com/blog/youtube-shorts-rpm-explained-niche-earnings)
- vidIQ creator example: own Shorts RPM about 13 cents per 1k across 2025 into 2026 — [vidIQ via rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/how-much-youtube-actually-pays-for-shorts-in-2026)
- India Shorts about ₹2–₹10 per 1k ([upGrowth/identitykit via search](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-india-guide-2026/)). Example Indian finance Shorts RPM of ₹35 ([fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-india-finance-rpm-2026)). This conflicts sharply with AIR's India Shorts RPM of about $0.01 (about ₹0.85).

### Inferences
- Even in finance, Shorts rarely exceed about $0.30–$0.50 RPM. For Indian audiences, Shorts revenue is negligible, at about ₹1–₹10 per 1k. Shorts are best treated as a discovery funnel to long-form.

### Gaps
- No reliable finance-specific Shorts RPM from a measured dataset was found. The ₹35 figure is an illustrative example, not a measurement.

## Q4. Seasonality (Q4 high, January low)

### Takeaway
RPM peaks in Q4 (November–December) and drops sharply in January. Estimates of the December-to-January fall range from 20% to 60%. AIR's measured median fell about 34%, from $2.35 in November to $1.56 in January, and AIR's peak was November, not December. The size of the swing varies by niche: gaming is steepest and health/wellness is nearly flat.

### Cited Findings
- AIR, 300 channels: pooled median RPM was $2.35 in November, $2.12 in December and $1.56 in January, about one-third lower — [AIR 2026](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026); [milx Q4 explainer](https://milx.app/en/guidelines/q4-youtube-earnings-explained-how-much-more-creators-make)
- CPMs frequently drop 20–40% from December to January ([fluxnote January drop](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-rpm-january-drop)). Others say 30–50%, or 30–60% depending on niche ([fluxnote by month](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-rpm-by-month-2026); [milx Q1](https://milx.app/guidelines/why-does-youtube-revenue-drop-in-q1)). One headline claims "December pays 3x January" ([fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-rpm-by-month-2026))
- By niche: gaming drops about 50% from December, the sharpest; health & wellness drops about 5%, almost flat — [fluxnote seasonal guide](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-seasonal-earnings-guide)
- Creator example: $14.23 RPM in December against $7.05 in January — [vidIQ via rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/why-am-i-making-less-money-on-youtube)
- Live-stream median RPM in 2025 rose from $2.31 in January to $5.47 in December — [gyre.pro](https://gyre.pro/blog/q4-youtube-rpm-and-why-a-live-stream-pays-most-in-november-and-december)
- India: Q4 RPM runs 50–80% above January–March in most Indian niches — [fluxnote India RPM](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-rpm-india-2026)
- Tax content has seasonal spikes, with higher rates in tax season — [outlierkit finance RPM](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)

### Inferences
- Plan annual revenue on a blended RPM. Q4 RPM is roughly 1.3–2x Q1 RPM.
- Finance and tax channels may have a second peak around filing deadlines (US: Jan–Apr; India: July ITR, March year-end), which partly offsets the January dip. This is plausible but only weakly sourced.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube or ad-industry figures quantify the 2025–26 seasonal swing.
- India-specific tax-season RPM spikes are not quantified.
