## 1. YouTube Partner Program (YPP) eligibility requirements

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026: full ad-revenue YPP = 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 public watch hours (12 months) OR 1,000 subs + 10M public Shorts views (90 days); a lower "fan funding" tier (memberships, Super Thanks, Shopping) opens at roughly 500 subs. On Aug 10, 2026 YouTube announced that from Feb 1, 2027 new applicants will need 8,000 *qualified* watch hours or 20M qualified Shorts views (1,000-sub bar unchanged; existing partners don't requalify).

### Cited Findings
- Standard tier (ad revenue): 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 public watch hours in last 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers + 10 million public Shorts views in 90 days — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/monetize-youtube-guide/)
- Lower / fan-funding tier: 500 subscribers + 3 public uploads in last 90 days + either 3,000 public watch hours (12 months) or 3 million Shorts views (90 days); unlocks channel memberships, Super Thanks/Chat/Stickers and Shopping, not ad revenue — [Tella](https://www.tella.com/blog/how-to-join-youtube-partner-program); [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-partner-program/) (secondary; sources word the tier slightly differently)
- Announced Aug 10, 2026, effective Feb 1, 2027: new applicants need 8,000 qualified watch hours (trailing 365 days) or 20 million qualified Shorts views (trailing 90 days); 1,000-subscriber requirement and Fan Funding tier unchanged; existing YPP channels do not requalify — [UpTube](https://www.uptube.io/blog/youtube-new-policies-2026); [Plumefy](https://plumefy.com/blog/youtube-monetization-requirements-2027); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/trending/youtube-updates-its-monetization-rules-what-creators-need-to-know); [PPC Land headline](https://ppc.land/new-youtube-creators-face-8-000-hour-bar-for-ad-revenue-from-february-2027/)
- "Qualified" watch hours = hours on public long-form videos, podcasts or archived live streams; excludes private/unlisted/deleted videos, Shorts watch time, ad time and unarchived streams — [Alan Spicer](https://alanspicer.com/what-are-qualified-watch-hours-and-views/); [aituber.app](https://aituber.app/blog/youtube-qualified-watch-hours-shorts-views/)
- YouTube reportedly justified the increase with platform growth (200B+ daily Shorts views; 1B+ hours/day watched on TV) — [UpTube](https://www.uptube.io/blog/youtube-new-policies-2026) (secondary quote)
- Passing thresholds does not guarantee admission: channels are human-reviewed against monetization policies (incl. inauthentic/reused content) — [Google support page via search](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)

### Inferences
- A new channel starting in late 2026 that reaches 4,000 hours only after Feb 1, 2027 will face the 8,000-hour bar; applying before that date matters.
- Faceless/AI channels get human review at application time, so the inauthentic-content test is effectively a gate, not just an after-the-fact penalty.

### Gaps
- Could not read YouTube's official Aug 10, 2026 announcement text directly; all 2027 figures are from multiple consistent secondary sources. Fan-funding tier country availability (India included at launch in 2023) not re-verified for 2026.

## 2. "Repetitious" -> "Inauthentic content" (July 2025) and the July 2026 clarification; enforcement and examples

### Takeaway
On July 15, 2025 YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" YPP policy to "inauthentic content" (called a clarification, not a new rule): mass-produced/repetitive content viewers see as spam is not monetizable, and it is judged at the *channel* level. Around July 16, 2026 YouTube rewrote the policy into three named non-monetizable buckets: (1) generic/template-based mass-produced content, (2) off-putting/distressing/emotionally manipulative content, (3) AI personas discussing sensitive topics (health, finance, legal). Enforcement escalated through Dec 2025-2026 with high-profile terminations.

### Cited Findings
**July 2025 rename**
- Effective July 15, 2025 the "repetitious content" policy was renamed "inauthentic content"; YouTube said it was a clarification, not a new policy; targets "repetitive or mass-produced content that users often consider spam" — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos)
- It is a monetization rule, not a platform ban; mass-produced content can stay on YouTube but not earn — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/7/youtube-updates-its-policy-to-demonetize-inauthentic-mass-produced-ai-generated-content)
- Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie (July 2025): change is a minor update to a long-standing policy; examples of what is flagged include narrative-story channels with only superficial differences between uploads and slideshow channels with the same narration; "YouTube welcomes creators using AI tools to enhance storytelling, and channels that use AI in their content remain eligible for monetization" (also pointed to YouTube's own autodubbing and Dream Screen) — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/); [Typecast summary](https://typecast.ai/learn/youtube-ai-monetization-july-15-ypp-update/); [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/blog/youtube-ai-slop-policies-faceless-monetization)
- Policy applies to channel as a whole: if YouTube "cannot clearly tell that you made the content", monetization may be removed from the entire channel — [Google support page (via search)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)

**July 2026 clarification (three categories)**
- TechCrunch (July 20, 2026, "YouTube clarifies policies around AI slop and upsetting videos"): policy now names three types of inauthentic content that cannot be monetized — generic/template-based content, off-putting or distressing content, and content where AI personas discuss sensitive topics like health and finance; channels dedicated to such content are removed from YPP *regardless of whether it is AI-generated* — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/) (read via search snippet only)
- Changes took effect July 16, 2026 and do not ban AI-generated videos; third category covers AI-generated personas on health care, finance, legal issues and medical advice — [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules); [TheDesk](https://www.thedesk.net/2026/07/youtube-clamps-down-low-quality-ai-slop/); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/new-youtube-guidelines-are-looking-to-end-ai-slop-and-clickbait-traps/)
- MoneyWise, relaying TechCrunch, phrases category 3 as "AI-generated representations of real people" on finance/legal/health being barred from monetization — [MoneyWise](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/youtube-ai-slop-inauthentic-content-monetization-rules). Note wording conflict with other reports ("AI personas" generally); exact scope should be verified on the Help Center page.
- Effective date varies by source: July 13, July 16 (most common), or "pages rebuilt July 9-14 with no changelog" — [noobclaw](https://noobclaw.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026/); [faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-rules-inauthentic-content-amid-ai-generated-video-concerns-12183810)

**Enforcement and named cases**
- Fake AI movie trailers: Screen Culture (India) and KH Studio (Georgia, US) — ads suspended earlier in 2025 after a Deadline investigation, reinstated after adding "fan-made" labels, then *terminated* in Dec 2025; combined 2M+ subscribers and 1B+ views; YouTube spokesperson cited "spam and misleading metadata" policies — [Gizmodo (ad pause)](https://gizmodo.com/youtube-pauses-monetization-for-2-huge-fake-movie-trailer-accounts-2000582621); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/tech/youtube-terminates-screen-culture-kh-studio-over-ai-fake-movie-trailers/); [Film Stories](https://filmstories.co.uk/news/youtube-has-deleted-two-major-channels-which-specialised-in-fake-film-trailers/); [Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/12/26/youtube-ai-film-trailers-takedowns/)
- Late Jan 2026: 16 channels from Kapwing's Nov 2025 "top 100 AI slop channels" list removed — ~35M subscribers, ~4.72B lifetime views, est. ~$9.8-10M/yr ad revenue; 11 fully terminated, 5 had content wiped (e.g., Three Minute Wisdom, 1.7M subs); YouTube spokesperson cited spam policy breaches — [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/youtube-cracks-down-ai-generated-content-1800213); [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2589980/youtube-cracks-down-deletes-47b-views-of-ai-spam-videos); [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-removes-major-ai-slop-channels-with-combined-9-8m-in-estimated-annual-revenue/); [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/tech/artificial-intelligence/youtube-gets-tough-on-ai-slop-ruining-the-party-for-million-dollar-brain-rot-video-channels)
- Kapwing found AI slop ~21% of Shorts shown to new users (figure varies across reports) — [Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/2589980/youtube-cracks-down-deletes-47b-views-of-ai-spam-videos)
- Claim that an automated system terminated ~50,000 "clusters" / 130,000 channels in six months by detecting shared AI templates — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/artificial-intelligence/how-google-machine-terminated-130000-ai-slop-youtube-channels-six-months-903645) (single source; unverified)
- Collateral damage: legitimate faceless human creators report being caught by automated flagging (TNW, June 15, 2026) — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/youtubes-war-on-ai-slop-collides-with-faceless-creators-who-built-the-platform-first/)

### Inferences
- Enforcement tools in practice: (a) YPP application rejection, (b) channel-wide demonetization, (c) for spam/misleading-metadata, full termination (Community Guidelines, not just YPP). Fake-trailer case shows labels ("fan-made") alone don't protect a pattern of misleading content.
- Programmatic pipelines (Claude Code scripts generating videos from a fixed template) are exactly what category 1 targets if the output videos differ only superficially. Variation in format, original research/data, and a recognizable editorial voice per video are the main defences.
- AI true-crime/"brain rot" story channels and "distressing" content fall under category 2 even if human-made.

## 3. Reused content policy

### Takeaway
Separate from inauthentic content and unchanged by the 2025/2026 updates: content that repurposes others' material (YouTube or elsewhere) without significant original commentary, modification or educational/entertainment value is not monetizable; also channel-level.

### Cited Findings
- Official: "no change to our reused content policy which reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos"; channel-wide removal possible — [Google support (via search)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550123/)
- Not allowed examples: clip compilations from shows with minimal narration; non-verbal reactions without commentary; content uploaded many times by others; (reported) read-aloud of others' text/articles — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/understanding-youtubes-reused-content-policy)
- Allowed examples: critical reviews using clips, reactions with substantive commentary, compilations edited to tell a story, dubbing/voice-over with meaningful added value — [Google support (via search)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/)
- Distinction: reused content measures transformation of others' material; inauthentic content measures mass production/repetition patterns — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)

### Inferences
- Stock footage + scraped article text narrated by TTS risks *both* policies: reused (text/footage not yours) and inauthentic (template). Original scripts, own data/charts, and programmatic visuals generated from original analysis reduce risk.

### Gaps
- Official example list wording not verified verbatim.

## 4. AI disclosure (altered or synthetic content label)

### Takeaway
Since March 2024 (enforcement tightened 2025), creators must tick "altered or synthetic content" in YouTube Studio when realistic content could be mistaken for a real person, place or event. Not required for clearly unrealistic/animated content, productivity uses (scripting, captions), or minor edits. Label itself doesn't affect reach; repeated non-disclosure can lead to label being forced, removal, or YPP suspension.

### Cited Findings
- Announced March 2024: creators must label "realistic" AI content; YouTube may add label itself where content can mislead — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/youtube-says-will-require-creators-label-ai-content-rcna143937); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/youtube-lays-out-new-rules-for-realistic-ai-generated-videos-154248008.html); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/102315-youtube-introduces-labeling-requirements-synthetic-altered-content.html)
- Exemptions: clearly unrealistic/stylized content, color adjustment, background blur, beauty filters — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/youtube-lays-out-new-rules-for-realistic-ai-generated-videos-154248008.html)
- Penalties for consistent non-disclosure: label applied by YouTube, content removal, suspension from YPP; label does not change ranking — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtubes-ai-content-disclosure-policy); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/391062/None)
- One source says mandatory enforcement in effect from May 21, 2025; another reports labels moved to more visible spots (2025/26) — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtubes-ai-content-disclosure-policy); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-shifts-generative-ai-labels-to-spots-viewers-will-actually-see/) (dates unverified)
- AI-generated content simulating an identifiable person's face/voice can be removed via privacy request — [HKCERT](https://www.hkcert.org/security-news/youtube-cracks-down-on-synthetic-media-with-ai-disclosure-requirement)

### Inferences
- For TTS narration over charts/animated or programmatic graphics, disclosure is generally *not* required (not realistic depiction of real people/events). A synthetic voice cloned from a real person, or photoreal AI visuals of real places/events, does trigger it. When in doubt, ticking the box carries no reach penalty.

### Gaps
- Reported May 2026 auto-labeling update unverified.

## 5. TTS / AI voiceover and faceless channels

### Takeaway
No YouTube policy bans TTS or AI voices; faceless channels are monetizable. What is penalized is low-effort template output — TTS reading scraped/generic text over stock slideshow at volume is the archetypal flagged format. Under the July 2026 rule, an AI *persona* presented as an expert on finance/health/legal is a specific non-monetizable category.

### Cited Findings
- Ritchie: channels using AI remain eligible; flagged examples are near-identical narrative channels and slideshow channels with the same narration — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/); [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/blog/youtube-ai-slop-policies-faceless-monetization)
- YouTube itself ships AI voice features (auto-dubbing) — [Typecast](https://typecast.ai/learn/youtube-ai-monetization-july-15-ypp-update/)
- Risky format examples cited post-July 2026: scraped article read by TTS over stock slideshow posted 3x/day; TTS over stock footage without editorial analysis — [noobclaw](https://noobclaw.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026/); [Kompozy](https://kompozy.io/guides/youtube-ai-content-policy) (vendor blogs; interpretive)
- A faceless narrator reading a script without claiming to be a person is treated differently from an AI advisor posing as a professional — [faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/) (interpretive)

### Inferences
- Safe pattern for the user's stack: original Claude-assisted *researched* scripts with human editorial judgment, TTS narration that doesn't impersonate a named human expert, programmatic data visuals (charts, calculators, worked examples), varied formats, sensible cadence. Avoid: an AI avatar "CA/financial advisor" persona; identical template across hundreds of uploads; scraped text.

### Gaps
- No direct primary YouTube statement specifically on "TTS voices" found; conclusions rest on Ritchie's general statements plus policy examples.

## 6. YMYL topics (finance/tax/health), misinformation, and India (SEBI/ASCI)

### Takeaway
Finance/health content faces three layers: (1) YouTube's July 2026 AI-persona-on-sensitive-topics monetization exclusion, (2) Community Guidelines on medical misinformation and scams plus advertiser-friendly limits, (3) for India, SEBI rules: unregistered creators cannot give specific securities advice or make return claims, can't use live prices in "education", and SEBI-regulated entities can't pay/associate with unregistered finfluencers.

### Cited Findings
- July 2026: AI personas on health, finance, legal, medical advice not monetizable; aim is protection from untrustworthy AI medical/financial advice by synthetic avatars — [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/new-youtube-guidelines-are-looking-to-end-ai-slop-and-clickbait-traps/)
- YouTube medical misinformation framework (2023) removes content offering dangerous medical advice or discouraging professional care; labeled AI content is not exempt from misinformation rules — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/a-long-term-vision-for-medical-misinformation-policies/); [YouTube policy updates page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10008196?hl=en)
- Oct 2025 policy change: enforcement against false claims that tobacco/nicotine are not addictive/harmful — [YouTube policy updates page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10008196?hl=en)
- SEBI Oct 2024 circular restricted registered entities associating with unregistered ones; Jan 29, 2025 circular: regulated entities (MFs, brokers, RIAs, RAs) barred from associating (money, referrals, IT integration) with unregistered finfluencers; educators must not give advice or performance claims — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/01/sebi-tightens-grip-on-unregistered-financial-advisors); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/social-media/sebi-cracks-down-on-finfluencers-with-new-rules-on-stock-market-education-55026.htm); [NLIU Law Review](https://nliulawreview.nliu.ac.in/sebis-crackdown-on-finfluencers-a-legal-and-regulatory-perspective/)
- Education content may only use market price data with at least a three-month lag (no live prices) — [Moneylife](https://mas360.moneylife.in/article/sebi-tightens-regulations-on-finfluencers-new-guidelines-for-educational-content-and-stock-market-advice/4724.html); [Prime Legal](https://blog.primelegal.in/sebi-finfluencer-regulations-2026-guide/)
- Enforcement example: Dec 2024 SEBI action against "Baap of Chart" (Mohammad Nasiruddin Ansari), unregistered YouTube/X market "expert" — [Equentis/NLIU](https://nliulawreview.nliu.ac.in/sebis-crackdown-on-finfluencers-a-legal-and-regulatory-perspective/)
- SEBI circular dated Feb 26, 2026: regulated entities and their agents must display registered name + SEBI registration number on social media content (YouTube included) from May 1, 2026 — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/business/sebi-mandates-social-media-disclosure-registration-number-must-be-shown-from-may-1-2026); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/banking-and-finance/social-media-disclosures-intermediaries/); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-mandates-social-media-disclosures-for-market-intermediaries)
- Proposed SEBI ad code would treat finfluencers with 500,000+ followers on a platform, and AI avatars, under celebrity endorsement rules (proposal, not adopted as of sources) — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/sebis-new-ad-code-brings-finfluencers-ai-avatars-under-celebrity-rules-155726.html)
- SEBI reported taking down ~1.2 lakh misleading finfluencer posts and expanding AI surveillance — [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/sebi-takes-down-1-2-lakh-misleading-finfluencer-posts-steps-up-ai-surveillance-152481.html) (date not verified)
- ASCI influencer guidelines require disclosure of material connections; for financial advice, qualifications/registration disclosure — [Agency Reporter](https://agencyreporter.com/indian-creators-navigating-sebi-asci-guidelines-2026/)

### Inferences
- For an Indian GST/tax/finance explainer channel: general tax/GST explanation is not SEBI-regulated (SEBI covers securities market advice); stock tips, "buy X", return claims, or paid tie-ups with brokers/AMCs are the danger zone. Avoid an AI "expert" persona; present as educational, cite sources (CBIC notifications etc.), add disclaimers, disclose any sponsorship (ASCI).
- Finance/tax is high-CPM, but under the July 2026 rule it is also where AI-persona demonetization bites hardest.

### Gaps
- Original SEBI circular PDFs not retrieved; placement details of the Feb 2026 disclosure rule conflict across sources. No specific YouTube "YMYL" ad-suitability document found for finance (YMYL is a Google Search concept, not a named YouTube monetization policy). Updated ASCI text not retrieved.
