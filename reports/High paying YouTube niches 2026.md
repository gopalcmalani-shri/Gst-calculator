# Aim English software tutorials at Tier-1 viewers

The highest-paying non-kids YouTube niches in 2026 are still the finance and B2B cluster: insurance, credit cards, tax/accounting, legal, personal finance and SaaS/software tutorials, each quoted at roughly **$8–$45 RPM** for US-heavy long-form channels. Gaming and entertainment sit at $1–$5. For this creator, though, the deciding variable is not the niche label but **who watches**. A US view is worth roughly 10–20x an Indian view, so the same GST explainer earns about ₹40–₹150 per 1,000 Hindi views but $10+ per 1,000 US views on a comparable English software topic. YouTube's **July 2026 "inauthentic content" rewrite** also matters directly. It explicitly demonetizes templated mass-produced videos and **AI personas discussing finance, legal or health topics**, which are exactly the shortcuts an AI pipeline tempts you toward. The best fit is a two-track plan. The primary money engine is an **English, globally targeted channel on accounting software, Excel and bookkeeping automation, and AI tools for accountants** (jurisdiction-light, $8–$25 RPM, strong SaaS affiliates). Alongside it runs a **Hindi/Hinglish GST, Tally and MSME-compliance channel**. That channel earns little from AdSense but is the best lead source for the creator's own GST/accounting SaaS, and both tracks steer clear of SEBI. Stock-market and investing content should be avoided. It is crowded with face-led CAs at 5–6.5M subscribers, it is legally fragile under SEBI, and it is the category where an AI-voiced "expert" is most likely to be demonetized.

Treat these numbers as ceilings. The only measured multi-channel dataset is AIR Media-Tech's 300 managed channels from May 2025 to May 2026. It found an overall long-form **median RPM of about $2.30**, and its Business & Finance median was only **$2.01**, although that figure rests on fewer than 10 channels ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)). The same dataset shows extreme concentration: the **top 10% of channels took 58% of ad revenue** and the bottom half took 4% ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)). There are three reasons for the gap. Many "RPM" tables actually quote CPM, which is before YouTube's 45% cut ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-rpm-benchmarks/)). The median channel ran ads on only **53% of its views** ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)). And insurance and legal inventory is thin, so high CPM does not reliably convert into high earnings ([1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-cpm-rpm-by-niche/)). A realistic planning figure for a well-run, US-heavy accounting-software or business-finance channel is therefore about **$8–$15 RPM**. The $30+ figures belong to high-intent US buying content such as credit cards, insurance and tax strategy. AIR's measured data does contain one useful signal: **Education & Science was its top niche**, which suggests long-watch-time explainers earn more than their blog reputation implies.

## Viewer geography outweighs niche choice by an order of magnitude

Audience country is the biggest lever available. AIR's June 2025 data across 3,000+ channels put **US CPM at $14.67 against $0.74 for India**, with Australia at $13.30 and the UK at $8.91 ([AIR](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-in-2025-10-countries-you-cant-ignore); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/08/youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025-how-location.html)). Indian long-form RPM typically runs **₹25–₹150 per 1,000 views** overall. A mostly US audience lifts the same creator to roughly ₹670–₹1,340 ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-india-guide-2026/)). Finance is the best-paying Indian niche, but estimates vary widely: ₹40–₹80 RPM in one estimate ([fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-india-finance-rpm-2026)) and ₹80–₹250 in another ([identitykit](https://www.identitykit.in/blog/youtube-rpm-india-niche-2026)). Finance content aimed at IT professionals and NRIs reportedly draws **₹200–₹500 CPM** because brokers and fund platforms bid aggressively ([fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-income-india-2026)). Viewer location matters more than creator location. NRI viewers in the US, UK, Canada and Australia pay out at roughly **$5–$12 RPM**, against $0.30–$1.50 for viewers in India ([youseo.app](https://youseo.app/youtube-monetization-india)). One worked comparison shows a 1M-view finance video earning about **$8–15K from US viewers versus ₹0.8–2.5 lakh from Indian viewers** ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-earnings-by-country-comparison)).

Language works through geography. English content in India is quoted at ₹80–₹250 against **₹25–₹80 for Hindi** ([identitykit](https://www.identitykit.in/blog/youtube-rpm-india-niche-2026)), with Hinglish in between at ₹50–₹150 ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-in-india-complete-guide/)). Sources disagree on the size of the finance-specific gap. One puts Hindi finance at ₹50–₹200 against ₹400–₹600+ for English ([smartcalc](https://smartcalc.online/calculators/youtube_earnings.html)), while another gives a much narrower $1.50–$4 against $2–$5 ([ytmoneycalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/how-much-youtube-pay-per-1000-views/)). The difference probably comes from how much of each channel's audience is outside India. No data source isolates CA, GST or income-tax content specifically, so Indian finance is the best available proxy. In concrete terms, 100,000 monthly Hindi GST views at ₹50–₹150 RPM earn roughly **₹5,000–₹15,000**. The same volume of English software-tutorial views from Tier-1 countries at $8–$15 earns roughly **$800–$1,500**.

Shorts do not change this picture. AIR's 274-channel sample found Shorts RPM of **$0.07–$0.20 in most niches**, about **3–14% of long-form RPM**. By country it ranged from about **$0.01 in India** to $0.33 in the US ([AIR](https://air.io/en/monetization/what-rpm-can-you-expect-from-shorts-in-2026)). Even finance Shorts rarely exceed a few tens of cents, so Shorts should be treated as a discovery funnel feeding long-form videos and the SaaS signup page, not as a revenue line. Seasonality adds a further swing. AIR's pooled median fell from **$2.35 in November to $1.56 in January**, about a third lower ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)). Indian niches reportedly run 50–80% higher in Q4 than in January–March ([fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-rpm-india-2026)). Tax content gets its own lift in filing season ([outlierkit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)), which could partly offset the January dip around India's March year-end and July ITR deadlines, although that effect has not been quantified.

## YouTube now penalizes the template and the AI persona, not the AI itself

Using AI is not the problem. Producing videos the way a cheap AI pipeline does by default is. On **15 July 2025** YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" rule to **"inauthentic content"** and called the change a clarification. Mass-produced or repetitive content that viewers see as spam cannot be monetized, and the judgment is made at the **channel level** ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)). Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie said channels using AI "remain eligible for monetization". He named narrative-story channels with only superficial differences between uploads, and slideshow channels with the same narration, as the targets ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/)). Around **16 July 2026** YouTube rewrote the policy into three named non-monetizable categories. The first is generic/template-based mass-produced content. The second is off-putting or distressing content. The third is **AI personas discussing sensitive topics such as health, finance and legal issues**. Channels dedicated to any of these are removed from YPP whether or not the content is AI-made ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules)). Reports word the third category differently: MoneyWise describes it as AI representations of *real people* ([MoneyWise](https://moneywise.com/news/top-stories/youtube-ai-slop-inauthentic-content-monetization-rules)). Check the Help Center text before relying on either reading.

Enforcement is real and escalating. The fake-trailer channels Screen Culture (India) and KH Studio, with a combined 2M+ subscribers, were **terminated in December 2025** even after they added "fan-made" labels ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/tech/youtube-terminates-screen-culture-kh-studio-over-ai-fake-movie-trailers/)). In January 2026, 16 channels from Kapwing's "AI slop" list were removed. Together they had about **35M subscribers and an estimated ~$10M a year in ad revenue** ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-removes-major-ai-slop-channels-with-combined-9-8m-in-estimated-annual-revenue/)). Legitimate faceless creators also report being caught by automated flagging ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)). The separate **reused-content policy** is unchanged. It removes monetization when others' footage or text is repurposed without substantial transformation ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/)). A TTS voice reading a scraped article over stock footage is therefore exposed on two fronts at once.

For a Claude Code pipeline, this means the safe path and the high-RPM path are the same. Use original, CA-checked scripts with a clear editorial point of view. Use **real screen recordings of Tally, the GST portal, QuickBooks or Excel**, plus programmatic charts and worked calculations generated from original analysis. Vary formats instead of running one template, keep the cadence sensible, and use a narrator who does not pose as a named or avatar "CA advisor". The altered/synthetic label is needed only for realistic depictions of real people, places or events, not for TTS over charts. Ticking it carries no reach penalty, but repeated non-disclosure can lead to YPP suspension ([AIR](https://air.io/en/youtube-glossary/what-is-youtubes-ai-content-disclosure-policy); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/youtube-lays-out-new-rules-for-realistic-ai-generated-videos-154248008.html)). Timing matters too. Current YPP ad-revenue eligibility is 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours, or 10M Shorts views in 90 days. From **1 February 2027**, new applicants will need **8,000 qualified long-form watch hours** or 20M qualified Shorts views ([AIR](https://air.io/en/trending/youtube-updates-its-monetization-rules-what-creators-need-to-know); [Plumefy](https://plumefy.com/blog/youtube-monetization-requirements-2027)). A channel launched now should plan for the 8,000-hour bar, which again favors long-form over Shorts. Applications are also human-reviewed, so the inauthentic-content test acts as a gate at entry, not only as a penalty afterwards ([Google support](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).

## Software tutorials face mid-sized rivals; investing faces giants

The competitive landscape points the same way as the RPM data. Indian personal finance is dominated by trusted, face-led creators: **Pranjal Kamra at about 6.5M subscribers** and **CA Rachana Ranade at about 5.4M** ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@pranjalkamra/); [socialcounts](https://socialcounts.org/youtube-live-subscriber-count/UCe3qdG0A_gr-sEdat5y2twQ)). A faceless AI channel cannot match their trust. Tally/GST tutorial leaders are smaller generalist "computer academy" channels that already use screen recordings, which suits a faceless format. Examples are Computer Tech Academy at about 522K, Happy Learny at about 864K and Learn with Vaya at about 181K ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@computertechacademy/about/index.md); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCFP8wzPazQR8TuByuWPlrQw)). A CA who has actually built GST software can beat these channels on depth. In global English, Leila Gharani's Excel channel shows a large ceiling at **3.1M subscribers** ([Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/leilagharani/achievements)). Hector Garcia's QuickBooks focus carried him to about 345K ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/hectorgarciacpa/)), and most CPA channels stay under 250K ([Feedspot](https://videos.feedspot.com/cpa_youtube_channels/)). Software and workflow tutorials are jurisdiction-light, so an India-based CA is not competing with US CPAs on IRS credentials. Claims of large faceless AI finance earnings, such as $13K/month or $50K/month, are all self-reported or promotional ([Becomeviral](https://becomeviral.com/blog/faceless-youtube-case-study-finance); [Cashflow Channels](https://cashflowchannels.beehiiv.com/p/50k-per-month-using-ai-voice-editor-youtube-dont-miss-channel)). Faceless finance channels also reportedly attract fewer sponsors ([Outlierkit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-channels/)).

Outside AdSense, the creator's biggest asset is his own product. A viewer who converts to a recurring GST/accounting SaaS subscription is worth more than any one-time commission. That makes the Hindi channel's low RPM acceptable: its job is lead generation. Among third-party programs, **Zoho Books pays 15% (18% above 200 sales) with a 90-day cookie** ([Lasso](https://getlasso.co/affiliate/zoho-books/)), although it competes with his own product. **QuickBooks pays up to 10%** ([UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-program-directory/quickbooks)), and Xero sets rates per partner through PartnerStack ([UpPromote](https://uppromote.com/affiliate-program-directory/xero)). QuickBooks and Xero fit the English channel naturally. Indian credit card affiliates pay **₹500–₹3,000 per approval** ([GroMo](https://gromo.in/blog/15-best-credit-card-affiliate-programs-in-india)) but are off-topic. Broker referrals are now small: Zerodha pays 10% of the referred client's brokerage plus reward points ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/tos/referrals?c=ZMPXVZ)). Paid courses on practical Tally+GST, return filing and ITR for freelancers have proven demand on Udemy and LearnVern ([Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/mastering-tally-9erp-with-gst-in-hindi/); [LearnVern](https://www.learnvern.com/course/tally-course)). Indian creators should also file a W-8BEN, or US-viewer earnings may face 24% default withholding ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-income-india-2026)).

## SEBI fences off investing; ICAI now permits education but not promotion

GST, accounting and software content falls entirely outside SEBI, and that is a structural advantage. SEBI's rules have applied since **29 August 2024** and were tightened by an October 2024 circular and a 29 January 2025 clarification. They bar brokers, AMCs, RIAs and other regulated entities from paying, referring clients to, or integrating with unregistered persons who give advice or make return claims ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/securities-law-update-sebi-imposes-restrictions-on-intermediaries-and-finfluencers/); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/markets/sebi-directs-regulated-entities-to-sever-ties-with-finfluencers-in-3-months)). Pure educators are exempt only if they avoid live prices and any data from the past three months used in a way that implies advice ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/sebis-clampdown-on-finfluencers-cannot-use-live-stock-market-data-in-educational-content-462704-2025-01-31)). A January 2026 consultation proposed changing that to a 30-day lag, but it has not been confirmed as final ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/01/07/sebi-mulls-30-day-lag-on-sharing-stock-market-data-for-investor-education-here-is-why.amp.html)). SEBI took action against the unregistered market "expert" behind "Baap of Chart" ([NLIU Law Review](https://nliulawreview.nliu.ac.in/sebis-crackdown-on-finfluencers-a-legal-and-regulatory-perspective/)), and a proposed ad code would treat **AI avatars** and finfluencers with 500K+ followers under celebrity-endorsement rules ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/sebis-new-ad-code-brings-finfluencers-ai-avatars-under-celebrity-rules-155726.html)). Investing content therefore carries both legal risk and weak monetization. Endorsements and sponsorships require ASCI disclosure of any material connection ([Agency Reporter](https://agencyreporter.com/indian-creators-navigating-sebi-asci-guidelines-2026/)).

ICAI rules are less restrictive than before but still bind. The revised **Code of Ethics (13th edition), effective 1 April 2026**, allows practising CAs to publish **educational videos and podcasts on YouTube**. Such content must contain no promotional claims, sensational or viral-style material, or influencer-style promotion ([ICAI](https://icai.org/post/prc-icai-press-release-12-12-2025); [taxroutine](https://taxroutine.com/icai-revised-advertising-guidelines/); [courtkutchehry](https://www.courtkutchehry.com/pages/blog/icai-allows-ca-firms-to-advertise-2026-code-of-ethics)). Earlier advisories told members to avoid the "CA" designation on social media ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/icai-prohibits-use-of-chartered-accountant-or-ca-by-members-while-using-social-media￼/216129)). An older announcement treats a practising member advertising coaching as possible indirect solicitation ([Studycafe](https://studycafe.in/icai-announcement-on-advertising-by-ca-engaged-in-coachingteaching-10716.html)), and one unverified source says website addresses are barred from educational videos ([taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1008895)). Several points remain unverified against the primary Code text: whether a practising CA may run a SaaS business, earn affiliate commissions or promote courses, and whether giving up the Certificate of Practice removes these limits. The prudent setup is to run the channels under a **product or company brand rather than "CA [Name]"**, keep the videos educational, and keep CA-service solicitation out of them entirely. These points should be confirmed with ICAI's Ethical Standards Board before affiliate links or course sales go live.

## Conclusion

The common advice to "pick the highest-CPM niche" misleads this creator in two ways. First, the high CPMs belong to audiences, not topics. Second, the July 2026 rules turned the cheapest AI shortcuts, templated slideshows and synthetic finance experts, into grounds for demonetization in exactly the niches that pay best. His real advantage is not access to AI tooling, which every faceless channel now has. It is proof of work that templates cannot fake: real screen recordings of software he understands as a builder, and programmatic visuals generated from correct accounting logic. That is what keeps the channel on the safe side of the inauthentic-content test, and it also produces the kind of content that SaaS advertisers and Tier-1 viewers pay for.

The evidence also has clear limits. No dataset measures RPM for accounting-software or GST content specifically. The only measured multi-niche dataset, AIR's, shows finance medians far below the blog figures, and the ICAI position on affiliate and product income is unverified. The first 6–12 months should therefore be treated as an experiment. Track RPM, watch time and SaaS conversion separately for each language and audience geography, and let those numbers decide where to put production capacity, rather than the published tables.
