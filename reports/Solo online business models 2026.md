# Build tools for accountants, not slop

For an India-based Chartered Accountant who already runs a GST/accounting software company and builds with Claude Code, the best one-person online businesses in 2026 are **small paid software tools for a global bookkeeping and accounting audience**: micro-SaaS, Chrome extensions and Excel/Sheets add-ins. The next best are **functional templates sold directly on Gumroad**, and an **English, education-only YouTube channel plus email list** to drive traffic to both. Every model in scope follows the same power law. The median subscription app earns under about **$50/month** a year after launch ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/es/article/news-apps-revenue-developers/)), **44% of self-published authors earn $100/month or less** ([Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-author-survey/)), and only **8–13% of Indian creators** earn anything ([BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-from-content-to-commerce-mapping-indias-creator-economy); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/only-4-5-6l-creators-in-india-are-monetizing-content-kofluence/story)). The winning question is therefore not "which platform pays most" but "where does this creator have an edge that AI cannot copy." Their edge is accounting domain knowledge combined with the ability to ship working software. That edge is worth most in B2B tools, matters less in templates and courses, and is worth nearly nothing in faceless meme pages, stock images, print-on-demand or prompt packs, where AI has flooded supply and platforms now demote, ban or demonetize AI output. Two constraints shape the whole plan. First, **ICAI rules bar a CA in practice from any business other than accountancy without Council permission** ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/ca-practice-with-full-time-employment-know-icai-restrictions-with-misconduct-cases-1439256)). Authorship and tutoring have general permission, but selling apps, SaaS or templates does not, and holding that permission ordinarily ends eligibility for audit (attest) work. Second, Indian payment rails are awkward: **Stripe India is invite-only** ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/india-exports)), so a merchant-of-record platform or Gumroad's direct INR payout should be the default checkout. The recommended starting portfolio is one global micro-tool line, one template storefront and one educational channel feeding an owned email list, with a book added once the channel has produced enough material.

## Every model pays the top 5% and starves the median

Across every category the research covered, earnings concentrate at the top, and AI-era supply growth is making the distribution steeper. RevenueCat's 2025 data shows the **top 5% of new subscription apps earning more than 400x what the bottom quartile earns**, and the bottom quartile makes **no more than $19 in year one** ([RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)). Median revenue per install after 60 days is **$0.31** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/the-state-of-subscription-apps-2025-launch)). Indie-SaaS trackers give a similar picture with weaker methodology: a median of about **$145 MRR**, roughly **70% under $1k MRR** and **6.1% above $10k** ([micro-SaaS statistics 2026](https://content-site-lovat.vercel.app/articles/micro-saas-statistics-2026.html)). Books are no better. In the Written Word Media survey of 1,346 authors, only **13% earned over $5,000/month** ([Written Word Media](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-author-survey/)). Etsy's FY2025 filing shows **$10.46B GMS spread over 5.6M active sellers**, about $1,870 per seller per year on average, with a far lower median ([Etsy 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm)). On YouTube, AIR Media-Tech's 300-channel dataset found the **top 10% of channels took 58% of ad revenue** ([AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)).

The "vibe-coding" boom adds a warning specific to someone building with Claude Code. Pieter Levels' fly.pieter.com, built with Cursor in about three hours, reached a reported **$1M ARR run rate in 17 days** from in-game sponsor billboards and paid planes ([levels.io](https://levels.io/full-multiplayer-python-websockets-game-ai)). But 404 Media's analysis argued that copycats will not repeat it, because Levels already had a very large audience ([404 Media](https://www.404media.co/this-game-created-by-ai-vibe-coding-makes-50-000-a-month-yours-probably-wont/)). Systematic data supports that view. Among apps still hosted on vibe-coding platform subdomains, only **7.6% report any MRR, against 43.5% across the wider index**, and the best of them earns **$155/month** ([BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/how-to-make-money-vibe-coding)). AI has made building almost free, which means **distribution and domain specificity are now the binding constraints**, not production capacity. The same data finds that selling tools to a defined professional audience is more reliable than consumer apps. That is the strongest signal in the research for this creator. They should build for accountants, bookkeepers and small-business owners, a group they understand and can reach, rather than chase mass-market consumer hits.

Security failures are the other side of cheap building. Enrichlead, a SaaS written with "zero hand-written code", shut down within days in 2025 after exposed API keys and a client-side paywall bypass ([Vibe Graveyard](https://vibegraveyard.ai/story/enrichlead-vibe-coded-saas-shutdown/)). A scan of about 130,000 vibe-coded sites found **one in five leaking at least one secret** ([RedHunt Labs](https://redhuntlabs.com/blog/echoes-of-ai-exposure-thousands-of-secrets-leaking-through-vibe-coded-sites-wave-15-project-resonance/?amp=1)). A CA whose main business is accounting software cannot afford a data-leak story under their name. Server-side licence checks, no secrets in front-end code, rate limits and hard caps on AI API spend should be standard on every side project.

### Software products carry the highest ceiling and the heaviest support

**Micro-SaaS and AI tools.** These have the best upside in the research, and the best fit with what they already do. Self-reported solo outcomes include SuperX, which reached **$1k MRR within about four months of a January 2025 start and $14k MRR later** ([Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/case-study/superx)), a solo founder at **$125k MRR** after narrowing to one customer segment ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/hitting-125k-mrr-as-a-solo-founder-by-doubling-down-on-the-right-segment-c4o2Tfs6mjdpip5yZhaO)), and Claude Code-built products reported at **$20k MRR in 83 days** ([Pulse.bot](https://www.pulse.bot/entrepreneurship/blogs/he-built-a-20k-mrr-mac-app-with-claude-code-663604dd-fccf-4295-9122-d4d814d6b9ba/)). All are unaudited and survivorship-biased. There are two structural costs. First, AI-wrapper tools earn about **41% more per user but churn about 30% faster** than non-AI apps ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/ai-apps-earn-41-more-per-user-but-churn-30-faster-revenuecat-finds/)), and every active user adds an inference bill, so credit-based or usage-capped pricing is safer than flat unlimited plans. Second, support burden is the real cost of SaaS. The research found no quantitative benchmark for solo support hours, but billing disputes, data-import bugs and "my numbers don't match" tickets are inherent to finance tools. A useful rule for a no-support-team side project is to sell **narrow, stateless or single-purpose tools**: files in, clean output out, with no stored customer ledgers. Examples are bank-statement-PDF to CSV converters, invoice-data extraction into QuickBooks/Xero/Zoho import formats, reconciliation checkers and VAT/GST-number validators for overseas markets. These limit both data liability and ticket volume. Payments are where Indian founders hit friction. **Stripe India is invite-only, and international payments need an Import Export Code** ([Stripe Docs](https://docs.stripe.com/india-exports)). Founders report rejections and delays from Lemon Squeezy and Paddle ([StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/lemonsqueezy-alternative-for-indian-saas-accepting-global-payments/)). A merchant-of-record such as Paddle or Dodo Payments, which handles foreign VAT and sales tax, is the practical default ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/stripe-alternatives-india)). Because their existing software company presumably already has Indian gateway relationships, the fastest route is to check whether its current gateway (for example Razorpay International) can bill foreign cards for the new product line.

**Chrome extensions.** These are the fastest software products to market. One developer had a paying user the day after listing ([DEV](https://dev.to/_af1eb8cb8faab148e10b3c/how-i-built-a-chrome-extension-in-3-days-and-got-paying-users-the-day-after-launch-47o0)). Solo-built Superpower ChatGPT reportedly runs at **$20–30k MRR on a freemium model with about 270k downloads** ([Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/how-i-built-it-20k-month-chrome-extension)), and a LinkedIn rewriting extension reached **$1,200 MRR** ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/i-finally-hit-1-200-mrr-and-it-wasnt-from-the-ai-thing-everyone-said-to-build-f21988c37a)). One developer built 20 extensions and **sold four in a month**, which shows that small software assets can be sold off instead of supported forever ([IndieNiche](https://indieniche.substack.com/p/he-built-20-chrome-extensions-sold)). The Chrome Web Store has no native payments, so developers use ExtensionPay, which charges about 5% plus Stripe fees and which one developer found depressed conversion at checkout ([DEV](https://dev.to/_350df62777eb55e1/real-numbers-freemium-chrome-extension-monetization-after-6-months-5hga)). Since **10 June 2025**, affiliate links in extensions require disclosure, user action and real user benefit, and violators can be removed ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/cws-policy-update-affiliate-ads-2025)). Extensions that sit on top of a large web app carry platform risk when that app changes its page structure. For this creator, extensions on top of browser-based accounting tools (QuickBooks Online, Xero, Zoho Books, the GST portal) are the obvious niche.

**Mobile apps and games.** These are the weakest software option for them. Hybrid-casual cost per install nearly doubled to about **$0.95 in 2024** ([GameDevReports/Adjust](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-mobile-games-insights-report)), and D1 retention across game genres averaged **27% in 2025** ([GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report)). For an India-based developer, the bigger issue is that rewarded-video eCPM is reported at **$10–25 on US iOS against $0.30–1.00 on Indian Android** ([Appalize](https://www.appalize.com/blog/monetization/admob-vs-alternatives-maximizing-your-ad-revenue)). An ad-funded game with mostly Indian players earns very little. New personal Google Play accounts must also run a **closed test with 12 testers for 14 continuous days** before getting production access ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)). Store fees are 15% on the first $1M on both Apple and Google ([Appbot](https://appbot.co/blog/app-developers-apple-google-small-business-programs/)). Google remits GST on Play sales to Indian buyers and **collects GST TCS from GST-registered Indian developers** ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9384608)). That creates an extra reconciliation task, though a familiar one for a CA.

**Web games on portals.** These avoid the testing gate and the store fees. CrazyGames is reported to share about **55–60% of ad revenue (70% of IAP)** and pays monthly via Tipalti above **€100** ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-crazygames)). Poki negotiates terms per title ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/publish-game-poki)). A vendor guide claims a well-performing casual portal game earns **$200–$2,000/month**, which is unverified ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/guides/web-game-monetization)). Fast AI-assisted HTML5 iteration suits this model, so it is a reasonable hobby-scale experiment. It plays to none of their professional advantages, however, and the portals control the traffic.

### Marketplaces trade discovery for most of the margin

**Digital templates.** For their profile, templates offer the best balance of effort to income: almost no support and fast to ship. Gumroad takes **10% on direct sales and 30% on Discover-marketplace sales**, and has acted as merchant of record handling global VAT/GST since 1 January 2025 ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/gumroad-fees-explained)). It **pays INR directly to an Indian bank** and does not support Payoneer ([Gumroad Help](https://gumroad.com/help/article/223-payoneer-and-gumroad)). That makes it the simplest payout route in the whole research. Etsy adds discovery but costs more: a $0.20 listing fee, **6.5% transaction fee** and **Offsite Ads at 12–15%** of attributed sales ([Craftybase](https://app.craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees)). It pays Indian sellers **only in USD to Payoneer** ([Etsy Help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/articles/6742925359255)), and reports conflict on whether new Indian shops are being onboarded ([ShipGlobal](https://shipglobal.in/blogs/etsy-new-sellers/)). Etsy's June 2025 rewrite of its Creativity Standards requires AI disclosure ("Designed by a seller") and **bans AI-generated patterns, recipes and assembly instructions** ([Value Added Resource](https://www.valueaddedresource.net/etsy-bans-ai-patterns/)). Documented income is thin: a freelancer reported about **$1,700 from Notion templates**, mostly via Gumroad ([Medium](https://medium.com/wealth-talk-%EF%B8%8F/freelancer-makes-over-1-500-selling-notion-templates-f00fe59d3fe9)). The arithmetic for Indian pricing is unforgiving. A ₹199 PDF needs about **559 Gumroad sales to net ₹1 lakh** ([DEV](https://dev.to/3dimli/india-has-80-crore-working-age-people-and-not-enough-jobs-here-is-what-smart-indians-are-doing-4c1d)). Templates should therefore be priced in dollars for a global audience. They should also be functional rather than decorative: bookkeeping workbooks, cash-flow forecasters, freelancer invoice-and-tax trackers, and VAT/GST reconciliation sheets with working formulas. A CA can make these correct where generic AI templates are not.

**Online courses.** Udemy cut instructors' subscription revenue share from 20% to **17.5% in January 2025 and 15% in January 2026**. The marketplace share of 37% on Udemy-driven sales is unchanged ([Udemy Teach](https://teach.udemy.com/enabling-investment-subscription-terms-update/)), and instructors describe steadily falling income ([Udemy community](https://community.udemy.com/en/discussion/163733/the-slow-fade-of-udemy-a-platform-turning-its-back-on-the-people-who-built-it)). Udemy **does not accept fully AI-generated courses** and requires disclosure of AI voice cloning and translation ([Udemy Support](https://support.udemy.com/hc/en-us/articles/30999984483607-Course-Quality-Checklist-Use-of-AI)). Skillshare has replaced its royalty pool with a discretionary Teacher Fund ([Skillshare Help](https://help.skillshare.com/hc/en-us/articles/12387192761357)). Indian instructors are paid by PayPal, Payoneer or Tipalti after filing a W-8BEN ([Xflow](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/get-paid-from-udemy-india)). Demand for practical Tally+GST courses is proven ([Udemy](https://www.udemy.com/course/mastering-tally-9erp-with-gst-in-hindi/)), but a course sold through their own YouTube channel and Gumroad keeps about 90% of the price, against 15–37% on Udemy. Teaching also sits closest to ICAI's "private tutorship" general permission (see below). Courses are best treated as a product line built on the YouTube channel, not as a stand-alone marketplace bet.

**Ebooks and KDP.** These are low-support, slow-earning and the safest model under ICAI rules. KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated text, images and translations, and **caps new titles at three per day** ([Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/amazon-adds-to-kdp-generative-ai-policy-caps-daily-self-publishing-uploads/)). Paperback royalties are 60% at $9.99 or more and 50% below that, minus printing costs ([Reedsy](https://blog.Reedsy.com/guide/kdp/amazon-self-publishing-royalties/)). The Kindle Unlimited fund reached **$69.4M in August 2026** ([KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/earn)), but the median self-publisher earns little. Non-US authors face **30% US withholding** unless they claim the India–US treaty rate of **15%** for copyright royalties (Article 12) on a W-8BEN, using PAN as the tax ID ([Winvesta](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/investors/w-8ben-form-for-indians-complete-guide-to-filling-and-filing)). Google Play Books keeps India on a **52% split instead of 70%** ([Google](https://support.google.com/books/partner/answer/9331459?hl=en)). A practical, well-indexed non-fiction book (for example, on running a small business's books with AI tools, or on GST for foreign SaaS sellers into India) is worth more as a credibility asset and as a container for channel material than as an income line.

**Stock assets, print-on-demand and prompt packs.** These should be avoided. By April 2025, **47.85% of Adobe Stock's image portfolio was generative AI** ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/ai-flood-on-adobe-stock-nearly-half-of-all-images-now-ai-generated/)), and buyer searches have excluded AI images by default since January 2025. Royalties are **33% for images, with floors of $0.33–$0.38 per download** ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/africa/stock/contributor/help/royalty-details.html)), and contributors report sudden drops in downloads in 2026 ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-38/has-anyone-else-experienced-a-sudden-drop-in-adobe-stock-downloads-in-2026-1630684)). **Shutterstock does not accept AI-generated contributor uploads at all** ([Shutterstock](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594676-ai-generated-content-contributor-faq)), and Pond5 and AudioJungle ban AI music and footage ([Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/learn/ai-music-distribution/does-pond5-accept-ai-music)). Redbubble's September 2025 tier system added platform fees that reportedly hit small sellers ([MyDesigns](https://mydesigns.io/blog/?p=3952)), and Merch by Amazon gates uploads by sales tiers ([BeBold Digital](https://www.bebolddigital.com/blog/amazon-merch-tiers)). PromptBase charges 20% on marketplace-sourced sales, with a reported median prompt price of $5.99 ([Cartmango](https://cartmango.com/sell-ai-prompts/)). An Indian artist said its share of their income shrank as competition grew ([Analytics India Magazine](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-trends/ai-artists-are-making-money-from-selling-prompts-rather-than-art)). In all three, an AI workflow gives no advantage because every competitor has the same one, and the CA's expertise is irrelevant.

### Audience businesses are distribution engines, not revenue lines

**Faceless Instagram and social knowledge pages.** For an India-based creator, these pay mainly through brand deals and affiliate links, and both pay little. Instagram offers India creators Gifts and Subscriptions but **no Reels ad-revenue share** was found. Gifts pay about **$0.01 per Star** ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/instagram-creator-monetization-by-the-numbers/)). Facebook's Content Monetization program still appears to be invite-only ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/?p=188326)). Its US$3,000/month Creator Fast Track is limited to the US and Canada ([TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-meta-creator-fast-track-facebook-creators-pay/)). X retired ad revenue sharing in September 2026 in favour of "Original Content Rewards", which pay on impressions from verified (Premium) users of original posts ([Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/elon-musks-x-ends-creator-revenue-sharing-what-happens-to-your-payouts-1.500634678)), and India has few Premium users. Indian brand rates for nano and micro accounts run from **barter to ₹5K per deliverable for nano and ₹5K–₹50K for micro** ([Kofluence](https://www.kofluence.com/?p=68620)), and faceless pages command the low end. Instagram extended its originality ranking from Reels to the whole platform in **April 2026**, so watermark-and-repost pages are now demoted ([Exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/influence-zone-news/push-for-original-content-signals-bigger-reset-in-creator-economy-154828.html)). The documented faceless case studies are self-reported and modest, such as a 3K-follower affiliate page at about **$400/month** ([AffLift](https://afflift.com/f/posts/194526/)). The faceless-page niche is also crowded with courses selling the model itself. The research also suggests that a practising CA cannot freely run sponsored, viral-style content (see the ICAI section). Social pages are useful only as a short-form funnel pointing to their own products and email list.

**YouTube.** This is the only platform with an open, India-eligible ad-revenue program, and the prior research established where it pays. US CPM averaged **$14.67 against $0.74 for India** ([AIR](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-in-2025-10-countries-you-cant-ignore)). A realistic RPM for a US-heavy accounting-software channel is about **$8–$15**, against ₹40–₹150 per 1,000 Hindi GST views ([upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/youtube-cpm-india-guide-2026/)). Policy risk is the highest of any channel considered. YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" rule applies at the channel level ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/)). The July 2026 rewrite explicitly demonetizes channels built on **AI personas discussing finance, legal or health topics** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). In January 2026, **16 AI-slop channels with 35M combined subscribers** were terminated or wiped ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-ai-crackdown)). The barrier to monetization also rises from **1 February 2027**, when new applicants will need **8,000 qualified long-form watch hours** instead of 4,000 ([AIR](https://air.io/en/trending/youtube-updates-its-monetization-rules-what-creators-need-to-know)). Vendor guides claim faceless Indian channels reach the old thresholds in **4–6 months** ([Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/faceless-youtube-channel-monetization-guide)), which is optimistic. AdSense is the cleanest payout of any model: a **USD SWIFT wire to an Indian bank in about 3–7 business days** ([Xflow](https://www.xflowpay.com/blog/get-paid-from-adsense-in-india)). Without a W-8BEN on file, Google withholds up to **24% of worldwide YouTube earnings**. With a treaty claim, it withholds **15% on US-viewer earnings only** ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/youtube-creators-in-india-have-to-pay-withholding-tax-on-us-earnings-111538.html)). For this creator, YouTube's main value is the screen-recorded tutorial that lands a QuickBooks or Excel user on their template or tool page. Ad revenue is secondary.

**Newsletters.** A newsletter is the owned asset that protects against everything above, because channel-level demonetization or a marketplace algorithm change cannot take away an email list. Substack **waives its 10% fee in India** but requires a connected Stripe account, and Stripe India is invite-only for new accounts ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/31a40575-f12a-4aa5-93bc-60e6ff2c0f23/)). Beehiiv takes 0% on paid subscriptions but requires a paid plan of about **$43–$49/month** to sell them ([DevOpsCube](https://devopscube.com/beehiiv-vs-substack/)). No credible median for paid newsletter income turned up in the research. The recommended approach is a free newsletter, used as the delivery channel for product launches rather than as a paid product in year one.

## AI is welcome as a tool and penalized as a factory

Platform policy in 2025–2026 converged on one principle: AI-assisted work with human expertise is allowed, while mass-produced AI output is demoted, capped, banned or demonetized. The platforms fall into four groups. KDP, Etsy, Udemy and Adobe Stock **accept AI with disclosure** but add friction. KDP caps uploads at three titles a day, Etsy bans whole categories, Udemy requires real instructor expertise, and Adobe hides AI images from buyers by default ([CineD](https://www.cined.com/ai-flood-on-adobe-stock-nearly-half-of-all-images-now-ai-generated/)). Shutterstock, Pond5 and AudioJungle **ban AI contributor uploads outright**. YouTube, Meta, Instagram and X **penalize unoriginal or templated output at the account level**. Meta removes monetization from accounts that repeatedly repost without meaningful change ([iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2025/07/15/meta-crackdown-unoriginal-facebook-content/)). Software marketplaces (Chrome Web Store, Google Play, App Store) and direct storefronts (Gumroad, Payhip, own-domain SaaS) **do not judge whether code or content was AI-made**. They judge function, privacy and payment compliance. No AI-content restriction was found for Gumroad, Payhip, Substack or Beehiiv, although this was not verified on their policy pages.

This sorting matters more than any RPM or royalty table. A Claude Code workflow is lowest-risk where the buyer pays for a result (a working tool, a correct spreadsheet) and highest-risk where a platform's recommendation system decides whether the output counts as "authentic". Collateral damage to legitimate faceless creators has already been reported ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)). An AI-assisted channel is therefore one automated review away from losing its income, however careful the creator is. Diversifying into products sold directly is risk management, not optional upside.

## India rails decide how much of each dollar arrives

Payout routes and fees vary more than headline royalty rates suggest. **AdSense** (USD wire) and **Gumroad** (INR bank deposit) are the cheapest routes because they avoid intermediaries ([Gumroad Help](https://gumroad.com/help/article/13-getting-paid)). **Etsy** (Payoneer only), **Udemy** (PayPal, Payoneer or Tipalti) and **Adobe Stock** (PayPal, Payoneer or Skrill, $25 minimum) pass through an aggregator ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/payments-earnings/payment-taxes/payment-requirements.html)). PayPal is the most expensive at about **4.4% + $0.30 per payment plus a 3–4% FX markup and 18% GST on fees**. Payoneer costs about **1% to receive plus 2–3% on conversion** and issues a free digital FIRA ([Skydo](https://www.skydo.com/compare/paypal-vs-payoneer)). Lemon Squeezy bank payouts to India depend on Stripe pre-approval; otherwise they fall back to PayPal ([Winvesta](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/lemon-squeezy-payouts-india-paypal-bank-fees)). In practice, someone selling $1,000/month through PayPal gives up roughly $80–$90 a month compared with Gumroad or a direct wire.

The tax treatment is familiar ground for a CA, with a few creator-specific wrinkles. On **GST**, receipts from foreign platforms and foreign buyers are generally zero-rated exports of services, where the recipient and place of supply are outside India ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-youtube-income)). Because their existing software business is almost certainly GST-registered, aggregate turnover is already above the ₹20 lakh threshold. They should therefore keep an annual LUT (RFD-11) in force to export without paying IGST ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-freelancers-consultants-guide-domestic-foreign-clients.html)), and file FIRAs month by month as export proof. Two open issues need attention. Affiliate commissions from foreign programs are exposed to the **intermediary place-of-supply rule** in IGST section 13(8)(b), and whether its proposed omission has been notified is unverified. Sales to *Indian* buyers through foreign marketplaces are domestic supplies, and Google treats itself as an e-commerce operator collecting TCS ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9384608)). On **income tax**, content creation is not a notified specified profession. CBDT's ITR code 16021 for "Social Media Influencers" sits under Profession but has not been notified, so most experts use **44AD (6% on digital receipts)** rather than 44ADA ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/cbdt-introduces-new-itr-code-for-social-media-influencers-sparking-debate-among-tax-experts-1428531)). From 1 April 2026 these provisions become **Section 58 of the Income-tax Act 2025**, with limits unchanged ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/analysis-presumptive-taxation-section-58-income-tax-act-2025.html)). If the side projects run inside their existing company, presumptive schemes do not apply anyway, and royalty and SaaS income simply joins the company's books. The **6% equalisation levy on online ads was abolished from 1 April 2025**, which matters only when they buy foreign ads ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/india-equalisation-levy-removed-tax-impact-2025-125040100810_1.html)). Treaty withholding of 15% on YouTube, KDP and stock royalties can be credited through **Form 67**, and its rule reference changes under the new Rules ([Karbon](https://www.karboncard.com/blog/platform-withholding-us-india-freelancers)). For FEMA, royalties map to RBI purpose-code group **P09** and software to **P0802/P0807** ([RBI](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/39710850/52220.pdf)). The code should be agreed with the AD bank and kept consistent with GST invoice descriptions.

## ICAI rules turn the ranking from "what pays" to "what is permitted"

The binding constraint is professional, not commercial. Under **Clause (11) of Part I of the First Schedule to the CA Act and Regulation 190A**, a CA in practice may not engage in any business or occupation other than accountancy without the Council's permission ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/ca-practice-with-full-time-employment-know-icai-restrictions-with-misconduct-cases-1439256)). ICAI enforces this. It suspended a member for 30 days for acting as a company director and engaging in business without permission ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/icai-suspends-ca-for-30-days-for-acting-as-company-director-and-engaging-in-business-without-permission-1433159)). The Council's general-permission list covers **"author of books and articles"** and **"private tutorship"**, with a cap of 25 hours of direct teaching a week for members who keep doing attest work. Members engaged in other businesses under specific permission are **ordinarily not entitled to perform attest functions**, although authorship is exempt from that bar ([WIRC-ICAI](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/announcement/Volume-5.pdf)). The revised **Code of Ethics effective 1 April 2026 explicitly allows practising CAs to publish educational videos and podcasts**, including on YouTube, but bars promotional claims and sensational, viral or influencer-style content ([ICAI](https://icai.org/post/prc-icai-press-release-12-12-2025); [taxroutine](https://taxroutine.com/icai-revised-advertising-guidelines/)). An older announcement treats a practising member advertising coaching as possible indirect solicitation ([StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/icai-announcement-on-advertising-by-ca-engaged-in-coachingteaching-10716.html)).

Mapped onto the twelve models, this gives three tiers. **Ebooks and KDP** fall under authorship and are the safest. **Educational YouTube, courses and a newsletter** fit tutorship and the 2026 Code, provided the content is educational and never solicits CA-practice clients. **Apps, SaaS, extensions, templates, games, Etsy shops, POD, affiliate-heavy and sponsored social pages** look like "business" and need specific permission. Incorporating a company does not get around the rule, because a directorship or partnership in a non-CA business is itself an occupation needing permission. There is one important nuance for this creator. They *already* run a GST/accounting software business, so their position is probably already settled in one of three ways: they do not hold a Certificate of Practice, they have Council permission, or they have an unresolved exposure. If either of the first two applies, new software and template product lines are an extension of a business ICAI already knows about, and the constraint costs little. If it is the third, regularizing their status is the first task before any new side project, and it matters more than any choice in this report. The full current text of the general-permission resolution, and any ICAI guidance on content creation or affiliate income, could not be retrieved in this research. These points should be confirmed with ICAI's Ethical Standards Board before launch.

The concrete starting portfolio follows from this ranking, assuming roughly 8–12 hours a week next to the main business. **In months 0–3**, they should first confirm the ICAI position. In parallel, they should launch an English channel under a product brand rather than a "CA [Name]" persona, covering QuickBooks, Xero, Zoho Books and Excel workflows and AI tools for bookkeeping, using real screen recordings and CA-checked scripts. The channel should feed a free Beehiiv or Substack list. They should also publish **three to five priced-in-USD accounting templates on Gumroad**, each matching a tutorial topic, and build **one stateless micro-tool** with Claude Code, for example a bank-statement-PDF to CSV/QBO converter or an invoice extractor that outputs accounting-software import files. That tool should ship as a freemium Chrome extension or web app behind a merchant of record, with server-side licence checks and capped AI spend. **In months 3–9**, whichever tool or template shows paid traction should become a small subscription product, and the losers should be killed or sold, following the extension-flipping pattern. **In months 6–12**, the best-performing scripts should be compiled into a KDP book (with AI use disclosed and a W-8BEN treaty claim filed), and a Gumroad course should be built if the list has passed a few thousand subscribers. Simple kill criteria keep the portfolio honest: no paid sale within 60 days of a tool's launch, or a channel below roughly 1,000 hours of watch time after six months of consistent uploads, means its time is reallocated. Given the distribution data, a sensible base case is **₹0–₹25K/month by month 12, with upside to ₹1L+/month** only if one tool finds a paying niche. Anyone promising more is selling the course.

## Conclusion

The AI boom has inverted the usual advice for solo creators. "Upload once, sell forever" marketplaces were the easy option in 2022. In 2026 they are the most crowded and most policed, because they reward volume and AI makes volume free. What AI cannot supply is verified correctness in a domain where errors cost money, and accounting is one of the few such domains where a buyer can tell immediately whether a tool works. For this creator, that means the side project is best treated not as a separate "creator business" but as **a lab for their core software company**: small global tools and templates that test features, the market and pricing without risking the main brand. A tool that wins can be folded back into the main product.

The main uncertainties are institutional, not commercial. ICAI's treatment of SaaS and affiliate income under a practising certificate, the status of the GST intermediary amendment, and how YouTube's AI-persona rule will be enforced against faceless finance education channels all remain unverified. Each could change which tier a model falls into. The portfolio above is built to survive any of them going against them. Its core income comes from products sold directly, its distribution is educational content that ICAI's 2026 Code explicitly permits, and its one irreplaceable asset is an email list that no platform policy can demonetize.
